# Full analysis assumptions

Recorded before preparing the analysis on 2026-10-11. Existing source files and reports remain the historical inputs; new results are written under `analysis/`.

1. **Duplicate transactions:** Use one row per Transaction_ID, retaining the first source occurrence only when the normalized booking, route, date, transaction type, amount, membership tier, booking channel, and payment method agree. Stop on conflicting duplicate IDs. Record removed rows and their financial effect. Booking_ID is not a customer identifier.
2. **Dates:** Apply `transaction_date_decisions.csv` by original CSV row, checking that the original date matches. Use the existing ID-informed conversions and confirmed TX9900561 correction. Retain the 11 documented M/D/Y fallbacks and quantify their uncertainty. Dates represent recorded transaction activity, not flight departure or earned revenue dates. Cost dates represent recorded operating cost activity.
3. **Comparisons:** Compare September 2024–August 2025 (Period 1) with September 2025–August 2026 (Period 2), two complete 12-month windows. These are analytical periods, not an asserted fiscal calendar. Calendar 2024 and 2026 are partial years and must not be compared as complete annual results. Month-to-month and year-over-year comparisons are descriptive; two years do not establish recurring seasonality.
4. **Money:** Treat all dollar amounts as one consistent, unspecified currency. Parse money to integer cents. Refunds retain their negative signs. Net recorded revenue includes ticket revenue, paid extras, change fees, and refunds. Currency, taxes, commissions, depreciation, aircraft ownership, financing, and other overhead are not supplied.
5. **Profit terminology:** Revenue less Crew, Food, Fuel, and Maintenance is a recorded operating contribution before gates and other unobserved costs. It is not net income. Joining transaction revenue to contemporaneous route costs does not establish flight-level profitability because flight IDs and revenue-recognition dates are absent.
6. **Categories:** Trim whitespace and normalize case. Map Website/Web to Direct Web; OTA/Online Travel Agcy to Online Travel Agency; App/Mobile to Mobile App; Corp Portal/Corporate to Corporate Portal. Empty, NONE, N/A, and Unknown membership tiers become Unknown; do not assume NONE means Non-Member. Empty payment methods become Unknown. Preserve the mapping in the preparation script.
7. **Bookings and customers:** Count distinct Booking_ID as bookings, not passengers or unique customers. A ticket transaction is not proven to equal one passenger or ticket sold. Analyze add-on attachment among bookings with a recorded ticket; assess unmatched transactions and inconsistent booking attributes before interpreting booking metrics. Membership and channel comparisons are associations, with route and time composition potentially affecting them.
8. **Operating activity:** A distinct Cost_Date plus Route_ID with one of each of the four cost categories is called a cost event. It is only a proxy for departure activity; no flight IDs, capacity, distance, passenger counts, or load factors are available. Revenue or ticket transactions per cost event are descriptive ratios, not fares, yields, occupancy, or unit economics per passenger.
9. **Gates:** Deduplicate gate charges by City, Gate_Number, and Year only if all repeated costs agree. For a separate illustrative scenario, map 2024-2025 to Period 1 and 2025-2026 to Period 2. This mapping is assumed, not established by source metadata. Allocate each shared gate's cost equally across its distinct listed routes within that period; keep costs of gates with no listed route unallocated. Report network gate costs including unallocated gates. Do not include this scenario in the primary contribution measure.
10. **Routes:** Treat each direction as a distinct route; optionally combine reverse directions into a market for comparisons. Do not infer that blank gate routes are zero-cost or infer their assignments. Use cleaned route codes from current CSVs.
11. **Interpretation:** Large differences and outliers are review leads, not proof of errors or causal effects. Decomposition and sensitivity exercises are arithmetic scenarios, not forecasts. No external market, competitor, economic, or benchmark claims are made.

Any additional assumptions will be added here before they are used.

12. **Comparable route-months:** Match the same directional route and calendar month across Periods 1 and 2 only when cost events are recorded in both months. Separately report Period 2 route-months with no Period 1 counterpart. This controls partial-period route coverage, but not changes in activity intensity, aircraft, or transaction-to-flight timing.
13. **Adjusted channel comparisons:** For descriptive Direct Web versus Online Travel Agency ticket transaction values, compare cells with both channels in the same route and recorded month, then weight both channel means using the same pooled ticket-transaction count per cell. This adjusts observed route/month mix only; it does not establish a channel price or commission effect.
14. **Sensitivity scenarios:** Apply hypothetical proportional changes to recorded net revenue, operating or fuel costs, refund outflows, or paid extras while holding all other recorded amounts and activity fixed. These arithmetic scenarios do not predict demand responses or avoidable costs. The uncertain-date alternative scenario changes only the 11 unresolved dates to D/M/Y, retaining any amounts that move outside the observed window in a separately labeled bucket.
15. **Membership attachment robustness check:** For a supplementary booking comparison, assign membership using only ticket transactions in the same period. Require all those ticket tiers to agree; label disagreement Mixed and retain Unknown. Measure extras on the booking in that same period. This avoids allowing an inconsistent tier on an add-on transaction to determine the cohort, but does not infer a customer's actual status or repair the source.
